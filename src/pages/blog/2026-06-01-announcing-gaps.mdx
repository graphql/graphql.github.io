The primary [GraphQL specification](https://spec.graphql.org/draft/) describes
the core behavior of GraphQL; the language itself, how the server validates and
executes queries, how errors propagate etc.

In addition to the main GraphQL specification, the GraphQL Foundation also
maintains secondary specifications such [GraphQL Over HTTP][over-http] and
[Composite Schemas][composite-schemas] which standardize how GraphQL should be
delivered over networks, or how distributed execution may occur respectively.

[over-http]: https://github.com/graphql/graphql-over-http/blob/main/spec/GraphQLOverHTTP.md
[composite-schemas]: https://github.com/graphql/composite-schemas-spec

Beyond these secondary specifications, GraphQL is otherwise (intentionally)
un-opinionated about how it may be implemented. For example, we do not specify
naming conventions in the schema or describe how caching should work in GraphQL
clients. Such concerns are left up to developers and library authors to decide
how they should handle this.

There are, you might say, some "gaps" in the official specification(s)...

Community-written specifications such as Meta's Connections Specification or
IBM's `@cost` directive have emerged to fill this void and establish convention.
They're written in the same format as our official specifications
and are similarly intended to be adopted across different frameworks and
organizations.

As an industry, we benefit from having existing, well thought-out specifications
to implement common problems, rather than having to reinvent the wheel each time
with competing standards.

This is a pattern we want to encourage more of!

## GAPs

At this year's GraphQL Conf, we announced **GraphQL Auxiliary Proposals
(GAPs)**. This provides the GraphQL community a centralized home to publish,
browse and discover auxiliary specifications:

👉 [https://gaps.graphql.org/](https://gaps.graphql.org/)

## How it works

1. Head to [github.com/graphql/gaps](https://github.com/graphql/gaps) and follow
   the contributing guidelines
2. Submit your specification as a `.md` file, written in
   [spec-md](https://spec-md.com/) format.
3. After merging, you own the GAP. You can freely update it without needing
   working group approval.

Once merged, your GAP gets published on the GAPs homepage with a permanent link
you can share. [gaps.graphql.org](https://gaps.graphql.org).

## Submit a GAP!

If you've got a battle-tested solution to a common GraphQL problem, or have
adopted a convention that you think others would find useful too, consider
writing it up as a specification and submit is as a GAP.

Help us fill in the "gaps" around the core specification :)
